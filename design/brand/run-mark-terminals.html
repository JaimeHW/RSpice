Mark refinement · Terminals

Run — giving the leads somewhere to land

Open-circle terminals on the leads, the way a schematic draws its ports. They make the symbol read more honestly at display sizes — and below 48 px they hand off to simpler cuts, because that is where the jank actually lives.

today
proposed · live tweak below

Where the jank comes from

thin leads
8-unit strokes render at 1.4–1.9 px in the 32–48 px range — anti-aliasing smears them into fog.
bare caps
Round caps just trail off; the eye has nothing to land on, so the leads read as noise instead of pins.
output stub
The 10-unit output lead all but vanishes below 48 px — the triangle loses its exit.
the fix
Terminate every lead deliberately: open ports at display sizes, filled junction dots at 24–32 px, and the proven clean cut at 16 px.

Terminal treatments

baseline

today — bare round caps, leads trail off

junction dots

filled tips; reads as solder joints; survives every size

recommended port rings

open ports, the EDA idiom; ring lighter than the lead so the triangle stays in charge

heavy rings

lead-weight rings; the tips start competing with the triangle

In schematic convention an open circle is a port and a filled dot is a junction — both honest idioms. Port rings say “connect here,” which is the better story for a simulator’s run button.
